# Limitations and Critical Reflection

The analysis demonstrates useful but modest unsupervised structure.

The most important methodological risk was structural missingness. Several employer-related variables were not applicable to self-employed respondents. A preliminary broad representation produced a K=2 split that corresponded exactly to self-employment status. This indicated that the model could learn survey-routing structure rather than the intended mental-health construct.

The representation was therefore redesigned. The primary model excludes employer-routing variables, while the workplace-support analysis is explicitly restricted to non-self-employed respondents.

A second limitation is cluster separation. The primary K=2 Silhouette Score is approximately 0.154. This is not evidence of strongly separated natural populations. The result is better interpreted as a broad tendency in respondent profiles.

The workplace-support model has an even lower Silhouette Score of approximately 0.102. It is consequently used as a practical contrast for organizational intervention rather than a definitive taxonomy.

A third limitation concerns PCA. The first two principal components explain approximately 28.73% of the primary representation's variance. The PCA plot therefore cannot be interpreted as a complete map of the data.

Finally, survey responses are self-reported and cross-sectional. The clusters do not establish causality, clinical status, or individual risk. Any organizational application should operate at an aggregate level and should not be used for individual employment or clinical decisions.

These limitations strengthen rather than weaken the analysis when they are explicitly incorporated into model selection and interpretation.
